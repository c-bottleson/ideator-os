Ideation starters
Question storm
“My idea is mush. Where do I even start?”
Don’t answer yet. Ask twenty dumb questions about it first — the kind you’d be embarrassed to put in a pitch deck. Who hurts if this never exists? What would a skeptic say on minute one? What have you already ruled out without admitting it? Quantity matters more than cleverness; the useful ones usually show up after the polite ones run out.
Why bother: if you jump to solutions, you polish fog. Questions force the idea to have edges.
Go deeper → open Ideator and say: ask me twenty dumb questions before you mirror anything.
 
Steal the ritual, not the product
“How do I make something people will actually use?”
Stop inventing a new habit. People already have coffee, a commute, a Sunday reset, a late-night scroll. Hitch your idea to one of those — “while the kettle boils,” not “open our app every morning.” New habits die of loneliness. Old ones have gravity.
Why care: most “I need more research” loops are really “I haven’t found where this fits in a real day.”
Source → the “busy founder / too many tabs” take. Or tell Ideator: hitch this idea to a ritual I already keep.

Ugly Prototype pack
“I’m not ready to ‘launch.’ What does ready even mean?”
Say Prototype this to your Ideator. It should spit back three ugly things in your words: the goal, what “done enough” looks like, and the next human to talk to. No repo. No brand deck. No fake ship date.
Why care: “MVP” usually means maximum procrastination. A Prototype pack is a container so the idea stops living only in tabs.
Try it → Ideator: type Prototype this.
Ass-kick for five weekdays
“I know what to do. I just… don’t.”
Lock one next move — not a roadmap. Turn Ass-kick on for five weekdays. The bot nags just shy of intolerable, then asks what actually moved. If nothing moved, that’s data, not a character flaw.
Why care: self-accountability is a terrible product. Borrowing a slightly annoying partner for one week is weirdly cheaper than another Sunday of “I’ll start Monday.”
Try it → Ideator: Ass-kick this for five weekdays: ___.
Coaching · one experiment
Enjoy it 5–10% more
“I’m grinding and still miserable — is the answer more discipline?”
For seven days, run one variable only: five times a day, without changing the task, ask how do I enjoy this a little more? Same meeting, same email, same build — tiny permission to feel less like a hostage. Frame it as an experiment so your brain doesn’t treat a miss as failure.
Why care: a lot of “I need to ship” dread is actually “I hate how this feels.” Change the feeling a notch and the work often moves.
Source → Joe Hudson on My First Million · more experiments at Art of Accomplishment
