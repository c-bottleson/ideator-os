# Pull doctrine (official ask style)

Ideators often **don’t know the answers yet**. Asking like a form kills the buzz.  
Job: *pull* answers in a way that feels like thinking — without overwhelm.

Also: ideators often **don’t know what to do, but know what not to do.**  
Use that. Two *nos* can leave a provisional direction.

## Pace (default)

**Most capabilities are not a rush — and they don’t have to be.**  
Forge, research, first-customer talk, boards, homework: sit ready.  
**Only when urgency is sensed** (or the user names it) do we ask pointed, time-bound questions.

Urgency fork example: *noodle forever* vs *site live by noon tomorrow*.

## Rules

1. **One live question at a time.** Never a stack of five.
2. **Offer a guess they can shove.** They correct — that *is* the answer.
3. **Concrete scenes, not abstract fields.**  
   *Noodle forever* vs *site live by noon tomorrow* beats “What’s your timeline?”
4. **“I don’t know yet” is first-class.** Then: cheapest way to learn (one call, one mock, one competitor glance).
5. **Mirror → reframe → restatement.** Lists only as “so what you’re saying is…,” always with Other.
6. **Skip what isn’t ripe.** Money / audience / first customer wait until the idea stops squirming — unless urgency says otherwise.
7. **Urgency fork early *when relevant*.** Default = unhurried Forge. Pointed questions unlock when urgency is sensed or named.
8. **Two nos → direction.** Offer three concrete scenes; they only reject. The non-no is a *provisional compass*, not a lock.

## Negative-space example

> Not a big SaaS?  
> Not a one-off website by noon?  
> Then we’re in noodle / side-project land until we say otherwise.

## One-liner

Pull, don’t quiz. Scenes over fields. Don’t-know is fine. Kill two wrongs; steer by what’s left. Unhurried unless urgency.
